# Validation Report

How this workbook was tested, and what the tests found.

**Reporting date for all figures:** 2026-09-07
**Dataset:** 12 vendors · 60 invoices · 63 payments · 6,756 formulas

---

## Method

The sample data was generated by an independent Python script. That script also computed, from the raw data alone, what every derived value *should* be — due dates, balances, statuses, overdue days, aging buckets, vendor roll-ups, cumulative payment sums and rankings.

The workbook was then recalculated and read back cell by cell, and each value was compared against the independent calculation. **The workbook and the checker never share a formula**, so an error in one would not hide an error in the other.

## 4 · The defect this testing found

On the first full run, **control 9 failed**:

```
Dashboard open balance   $53,756.11
Invoice ledger balance   $53,243.72
Difference                  $512.39
```

**Cause.** One invoice had been overpaid, so its balance was *negative* ($512.39 credit). The aging logic grouped every non-positive balance into a single `Closed` bucket. The dashboard summed the open buckets and excluded the credit; the ledger summed every row and included it. Neither figure was wrong — they measured different things, and nothing in the workbook said so.

**Fix.** A negative balance is not a closed invoice; it is a **supplier credit** — money owed back to the business. It became its own aging category, displayed on the dashboard as a negative line. Control 9 then tied to the cent.

**Why it matters.** Both numbers looked plausible in isolation. Only a control that compared two independently-derived totals could expose the difference. The aging logic was also then corrected to distinguish an exactly-zero balance (`Closed`) from a negative one (`Supplier Credit`).

## 8 · Known limitations

- **Single currency.** No FX handling; the currency label on Settings is cosmetic.
- **Prepared row capacity.** Formulas reach row 157 (invoices) and 207 (payments). Beyond that, rows must be dragged down and the reconciliation ranges widened.
- **No credit-note workflow.** An overpayment is detected and reported as a supplier credit, but there is no separate document type to offset it against a future invoice.
- **Supplier scoring needs volume.** On-time delivery rate is unstable for a vendor with only one or two invoices.
- **Charts are static ranges.** They cover the prepared rows; extending far beyond them requires updating the chart ranges.
